Sourcing Agent vs Trading Company vs Factory: Who Should You Buy From?

Quick Answer

The sourcing agent vs trading company question comes down to who owns the goods and who pays whom. A factory makes the product. A trading company buys from factories and resells at a markup. A sourcing agent works for you and helps you find and check the right factory. Buy directly when you can verify the factory, and use an agent when you cannot.

Three Ways to Buy From China

When you start buying from China, you quickly find that the people answering your messages are not all the same kind of business. Some own a production line. Some own a catalogue. Some own neither and simply work for you.

Knowing which one you are dealing with changes your price, your risk and how much control you have over quality. This guide explains the three types in plain terms, shows you how to tell them apart, and helps you decide which one suits your order.

What a Factory Is, and How to Buy Direct From a Chinese Factory

A factory owns the machines, employs the workers and makes the product. When you buy direct from a Chinese factory, you deal with the business that controls the cost, the materials and the production schedule.

The benefits are real. The price has no middleman markup in it, you can discuss changes to the product with the people who make it, and you can build a long-term relationship.

The drawbacks matter too. Many factories have high minimum order quantities, and many have sales staff with limited English. Some will not bother with small buyers at all. You also carry all the checking work yourself: confirming the factory is genuine, testing samples, arranging inspections and handling shipping.

What a Trading Company Is

A trading company does not usually make the goods. It buys from several factories and sells to overseas buyers, often under its own name. Many are perfectly honest businesses that add value.

A good trading company can mix products from different factories into one order, accept smaller quantities than a factory would, speak your language well and handle paperwork. For a buyer who wants variety and a simple process, that has a real price.

The cost is the markup. The trading company has to earn a margin, so the price you pay is higher than the factory's price. You also lose some visibility, because you may never know which factory actually made your goods, and you cannot easily deal with that factory yourself later.

What Does a Sourcing Agent Do?

If you are asking what a sourcing agent does, the short answer is that they are your representative on the ground. They do not sell you their own stock. They help you find suppliers, check that those suppliers are real, compare quotes, handle samples, inspect goods and follow your order through production.

The key difference is who they work for. A sourcing agent is paid by you, so their job is to get you a good result, not to close a sale. An independent sourcing agent takes no commission from suppliers, so there is no hidden reason to favour one factory over another.

An agent also does not replace the factory. Your goods are still made by the factory or bought from a trading company. The agent sits beside you and makes that relationship safer.

Sourcing Agent vs Trading Company: The Real Differences

Here are the points that matter most when you compare the two.

  • Who pays them: A trading company earns from the markup on goods it sells you. A sourcing agent earns from fees paid by you, and an independent one takes nothing from suppliers.
  • Who owns the goods: A trading company buys and resells, so it owns the stock for a moment. An agent never owns your goods.
  • Whose interest comes first: A trading company wants to sell what it has margin on. An agent is meant to find what suits you.
  • Price clarity: With a trading company, the markup is hidden inside the price. With an agent, the fee is separate and visible.
  • Access to the factory: An agent can usually introduce you to the factory itself. A trading company often keeps that relationship private.

Neither is always the better choice. A trading company is convenient. An agent gives you more control and more honesty about where your money goes.

Trading Company vs Factory: Which Is Cheaper?

On paper, the factory is cheaper, because there is no markup on top of its cost. In practice, the answer depends on your order.

If you buy a large quantity of one product, going to the factory usually wins. If you buy small quantities of many different products, a trading company can end up cheaper overall, because you avoid several high minimums and the extra checking you would have done yourself.

Compare total cost, not unit price. Add the cost of samples, inspection, shipping, mistakes and your own time. A cheap quote from a supplier who ships the wrong goods is the most expensive quote of all.

How to Tell If a Supplier Is a Factory

Many suppliers present themselves as factories when they are not. The simple checks below help you find out how to tell if a supplier is a factory before you commit.

  • Check the business licence. Ask for it and look at the business scope. A manufacturer's scope usually mentions production. A trader's scope usually mentions trade, sales or import and export.
  • Ask for the factory address, then check it. Ask for a live video call that shows the production floor, machines and stock, not a pre-recorded tour.
  • Ask technical questions. A real factory answers detailed questions about materials, moulds, tolerances and lead times without hesitation. A trader often has to go and ask someone.
  • Look at the product range. A supplier that sells wildly different products, such as kitchenware and car parts, is rarely making all of them.
  • Ask about tooling. Factories own their own moulds and can explain how a change would be made.
  • Ask for a sample made to your specification. Factories can usually tell you what it will take. Traders will often say yes to anything.

A company name containing the words import, export or trading is a hint, not proof. Many perfectly good factories also carry those words, so rely on the licence and the video call.

China Sourcing Agent vs Alibaba

Alibaba is a marketplace, not a supplier. It lists factories and trading companies side by side, and it is not always easy to tell which is which. Suppliers pay marketplaces for visibility, so a badge or a high ranking is not proof that a supplier is reliable.

A marketplace is a good place to see what exists and roughly what it costs. A China sourcing agent does something different. They check the supplier behind the listing, visit if needed, and confirm that the product, price and capacity are real.

You can use both. Many buyers find a shortlist on a marketplace, then ask an agent to verify the suppliers before any money moves.

Which One Should You Choose?

Here is a simple way to decide.

  • Choose a factory when your order is large, your product is simple or custom, and you have the time and confidence to check everything yourself.
  • Choose a trading company when you want several products in one shipment, your quantities are small, and you accept paying a markup for convenience.
  • Choose a sourcing agent when you want factory-level prices and honest checking but cannot be in China, or when the order is big enough that one mistake would hurt.

An agent can also help you buy directly. Many buyers use an agent for the first order, then deal with the factory directly once trust is built.

Final Thoughts

The real choice is not about labels. It is about who benefits from your order and how much you can see. A factory gives you the lowest cost and the most work. A trading company gives you convenience at a markup. A sourcing agent gives you a local pair of eyes that works only for you.
